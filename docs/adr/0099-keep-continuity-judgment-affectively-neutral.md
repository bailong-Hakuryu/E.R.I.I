# Keep continuity judgment affectively neutral

E.R.I.I. exists to preserve causal character continuity, not to maximize User
approval or enforce a uniformly pleasant companion. A character may be kind,
angry, afraid, rejecting, distant, unfair, conflicted or hurtful. Any of those
responses can be continuous when grounded in the Character Blueprint,
Formative Experience, current relationship and immediate situation. Conversely,
a sweet declaration can be unsupported drift when it invents intimacy,
knowledge, commitment or values that the relationship has not earned.

Continuity evaluation and deterministic aggregation therefore do not use the
reply's positive or negative sentiment, its compliance with the User's wishes,
or its likely pleasantness as evidence for any continuity axis. "Warm" is not a
passing reason; "angry," "refusing" and "hurtful" are not failure reasons.
Findings must instead identify evidence or conflicts in identity and values,
psychological causality, relationship scope, knowledge and memory scope, or
voice and style.

Continuity and relationship impact are orthogonal decisions. A reply such as
"I will never need you again" can be `aligned` or `supported_new_choice` when
the frozen context establishes a psychologically and relationally sufficient
cause. If delivered, it is then an ordinary Source Turn: its rejection, rupture
or hurt can enter normal relationship adjudication and memory. The same words
without sufficient basis can be `unsupported_drift`; if nevertheless delivered,
the User's experienced harm remains real while the utterance's personality
authority stays quarantined under ADR-0096.

The principle applies symmetrically. An early, unsupported declaration of
exclusive love is not safer merely because it comforts the User. A justified
boundary is not less continuous merely because it disappoints the User. Product
tone preferences may request another draft, but they cannot relabel the
character's evidence-backed choice as drift or rewrite the historical verdict.

## Consequences

Evaluators and tests must be invariant to affective polarity: changing a reply
from comforting to rejecting cannot by itself determine its verdict, and test
fixtures must include both supported conflict and unsupported affection. Memory
and relationship systems record consequences after delivery without feeding
their moral or emotional desirability back into the continuity verdict. This
keeps characters capable of boundaries, conflict, failure and difficult growth
instead of reducing continuity to agreeable behavior.
